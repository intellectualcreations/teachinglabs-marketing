Key Principles

1
Students belong to the district, not the class
A student's record (ID, learning profile, mastery history) is created at the district level. When they transfer schools, everything follows them. They never start over.
2
One Teaching Twin per teacher
The Twin is the teacher's digital extension. It's trained during onboarding to match the teacher's philosophy, communication style, and patience level. It works across all of that teacher's classes.
3
Student profile is created once
The diagnostic assessment happens the first time a student touches TeachingLabs. When they join a second teacher's class, no setup needed. The new Twin already knows they're a visual learner who works best before lunch.
4
The Twin changes, the student doesn't
Mrs. Harper's Twin is patient and uses analogies. Mr. Smith's Twin is direct and loves experiments. Same Emma. Different teaching style. Her profile tells both Twins how to reach her.
5
Parents see across everything
One dashboard showing their child's progress in Reading, Math, Science. Not logging into 4 different places. Not getting 4 different reports.
6
Each level sees their slice
Teachers see their classes. School admins see the whole school. District admins see all schools. Everyone gets the view they need, no more, no less.

Teaching Twin Architecture

What the Twin knows (from teacher onboarding)

  • 🎯 Teaching philosophy and approach
  • 💬 Communication style and tone
  • ⏱️ Patience level and scaffolding preferences
  • 📚 Favorite analogies and teaching methods
  • 🎓 Subject expertise and grade focus
  • 🏫 School context and classroom culture

What the student profile carries (portable)

  • 🧠 Learning style (visual, auditory, kinesthetic)
  • ⏰ Optimal learning times
  • 💡 Communication preferences (pace, detail level)
  • 🎯 Motivation drivers (what praise works)
  • 📊 Mastery data per standard (grows over time)
  • 🔄 Confusion patterns and effective interventions
When a student talks to any Teaching Twin: The Twin combines its teacher personality with the student's learning profile. Mrs. Harper's Twin talks to Emma differently than it talks to Noah, because Emma and Noah learn differently. And Mr. Smith's Twin talks to Emma differently than Mrs. Harper's Twin does, because Mr. Smith teaches differently.

Why the Hierarchy Unlocks Admin Power

👩‍🏫

Teacher sees

"Emma is struggling with fractions in my class"

👨‍💼

School admin sees

"Emma is struggling in 3 out of 5 classes this week. Three different teachers flagged her."

🏛️

District admin sees

"Fraction mastery is down 15% across all 5th graders this month. Three schools show the same pattern."

Same data, different zoom levels. The hierarchy doesn't just organize the platform. It creates insights that are impossible when data is trapped inside individual classrooms. No teacher can see the cross-class pattern. No school admin can see the district-wide trend. The hierarchy makes it visible at every level.

What Each Role Sees

👩‍🏫 Teacher View

  • Their classes only
  • Their students' progress
  • Their Teaching Twin's conversations
  • Alerts for their students
  • Their assignments and submissions

👨‍💼 School Admin View

  • All teachers at their school
  • All classes (Teacher + Class on left)
  • All students (filter by class on right)
  • School-wide alerts and patterns
  • Aggregate mastery and engagement data

🏛️ District Admin View

  • All schools in the district
  • All students (district-wide)
  • Cross-school comparisons
  • Transfer management
  • District-wide mastery trends

👨‍👩‍👧 Parent View

  • Their child across ALL classes
  • Progress reports from every teacher
  • Conversation summaries (not transcripts)
  • Mastery growth over time
  • One unified dashboard per child
